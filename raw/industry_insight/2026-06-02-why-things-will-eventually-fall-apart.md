# Why Things Will Eventually Fall Apart

## Part I: The Math

Gary Marcus presents a five-point argument about AI's economic unsustainability:

1. Companies treat AI like a winner-take-all market (similar to web search)
2. Everyone builds equivalent solutions with identical datasets
3. No competitive moat exists
4. Without market dominance, monopoly pricing becomes impossible
5. Intense competition forces commodity pricing and unprofitable margins

## Part II: The Psychology

Marcus notes growing awareness of these problems:

- His X post questioning AI's viability reached 750,000+ viewers with majority agreement
- Financial analysts including Steve Eisman and economist Julie Garran publicly share concerns
- Reports question corporate ROI from AI investments
- Anthropic ended unlimited token pricing, signaling market shift

## The Core Concern

Marcus argues "the economics don't make sense" and worries retail investors and index funds face significant losses. He quotes economist Brad DeLong's assessment that neither Anthropic nor OpenAI show "a plausible, well-specified path" toward sustainable high-margin businesses given competitive pressures and unreliable technology performance.

His prediction: 2026 will be remembered as the year when retail investors got stuck holding unprofitable AI company shares.
